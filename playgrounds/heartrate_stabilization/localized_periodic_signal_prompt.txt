We are analyzing a stabilized grayscale video containing a small user-defined
ROI. The goal is to determine whether there is a localized, time-periodic
luminance source in the 1.5-3.5 Hz band, and to extract a reliable time-domain
event series if such a source exists.

Do not assume any semantic meaning for the ROI. Treat this as a signal-processing
problem with possible nuisance sources: residual registration error, boundary
motion, local contrast edges, global illumination drift, camera noise,
compression or decoding artifacts, dropout frames, and mask/ROI geometry
artifacts.

Think deeply about a robust pipeline that separates these tasks:

1. Pixel candidate discovery:
   - Use per-pixel time series inside a candidate mask or bounding box.
   - Score pixels by spectral evidence in 1.5-3.5 Hz, but do not use spectral
     power itself as the final event-rate estimate.
   - Compare whole-recording scores to chunked scores.
   - Prefer pixels whose band-limited evidence is stable across chunks.
   - Penalize or exclude pixels near usable-mask boundaries and high-contrast
     edges.

2. Spatial validation:
   - Determine whether selected pixels form a compact spatial cluster or are
     scattered/edge dominated.
   - Quantify distance to candidate-mask boundary, connected-component size,
     centroid stability, and score concentration.
   - Compare selected pixels against null models such as shuffled time, phase-
     randomized traces, random interior pixels, and boundary-only pixels.

3. Time-domain extraction:
   - Build a luminance trace from selected pixels only after pixel selection.
   - Use temporal filtering only to isolate the band of interest.
   - Detect events in the time domain from the selected-pixel trace.
   - Enforce interval plausibility from the target band.
   - Report event timing uncertainty and rejected intervals.

4. Artifact controls:
   - Explicitly handle all-black/dropout frames as invalid samples.
   - Test whether broad spatial averages, image edges, or global intensity
     components explain the apparent rhythm.
   - Use PCA/ICA/robust regression to remove global or boundary-correlated
     components before pixel selection.
   - Compare results before and after hard erosion of the candidate mask.

5. Deliverables:
   - A ranked list of proposed analyses.
   - Diagnostic plots/videos that distinguish localized periodic signal from
     boundary or global motion.
   - Concrete acceptance/rejection criteria for trusting the extracted event
     series.
   - Minimal code-level changes needed to test the most informative next
     analysis.

Be skeptical. The desired outcome is not to force a positive result, but to
identify whether a localized, coherent, time-domain signal exists and whether its
event timings are trustworthy.
